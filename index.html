    Coconut flour is not flour

    1. It drinks liquid. Swap roughly 1 part coconut flour for 4 parts wheat flour, never 1:1.
    2. Add one egg per 2 tbsp of coconut flour, or the whole thing turns to sand.
    3. Let batter sit 3 to 5 minutes before cooking. It thickens a lot in that time — judge it then, not before.
    4. Low and slow. It browns and dries faster than wheat, so drop oven temp about 15°C.
    5. Stevia has no bulk, unlike sugar. If a bake tastes flat, it usually needs more salt or cinnamon, not more sweetener.
    6. Weights beat spoons. Coconut flour packs differently every scoop, so 1 tbsp lands anywhere from 6 to 9 g — enough to turn a batter into a dough.

    Converting your own recipes: coconut flour 1 tbsp ≈ 7 g · cocoa 1 tbsp ≈ 5 g · peanut butter 1 tbsp ≈ 16 g · yogurt 1 tbsp ≈ 15 g · butter or oil 1 tbsp ≈ 14 g · desiccated coconut 1 tbsp ≈ 6 g · honey 1 tbsp ≈ 21 g
